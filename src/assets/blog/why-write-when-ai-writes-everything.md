I am starting this blog at a strange moment. Tools can draft emails, specs, and blog posts in seconds. A recruiter or a client can paste my resume into a model and get a summary before they finish their coffee. So why add another page of words to the internet?

I do not have a grand theory. I have sixteen years of shipping software, a staff role mindset, and a habit of caring about clarity. I led frontend for an agentic AI platform at Unique AG. I co-designed SAP FSM's extensibility concept. I am building Shellui, an open-source microfrontend shell, from Zürich. Those facts already live on this site and in my resume. None of them require a blog to be true.

Still, I want a place that is mine. Project pages here are case studies: problem, role, stack, result. The resume is a timeline. Neither format leaves room for the messy middle, the tradeoffs I rejected, or the question I am still chewing on. A blog is where I can show how I think, not only what I shipped.

AI text is fast and often fluent. It is also anonymous. It averages the web. When I hire or when I choose a freelance collaborator, I look for judgment: what they notice, what they skip, what they push back on. I cannot see that in generated prose. I see it when someone writes from their own constraints, with their own name on the line.

I will not pretend models are useless. I use them in my daily work like any other tool in the stack. They help me explore APIs, sketch tests, and rewrite a clumsy paragraph. They do not replace the work of deciding what belongs in production, what the user sees first, or what we refuse to ship. That decision layer is still human, and it is still the job.

Writing in my own voice is a way to practice that layer in public. Short posts. No invented war stories. If I mention a project, you can click through to the page on this site. If I am unsure, I will say so. If a post ages badly, I would rather update it or write a follow-up than hide behind polished vagueness.

I also miss the slower rhythm of text. Social feeds reward hot takes. I prefer sentences that hold up when someone reads them six months later. RSS still exists. I will publish a feed for this blog for anyone who reads that way, including future me.

So yes, it still makes sense to write, even now. Not because the world needs more content, but because trust is built from specific, accountable words. I am available for staff-level engineering roles and freelance work, based in Zürich. If something here resonates, download my resume or email me. This blog is one more way to see whether we would work well together.

That is enough for a first post. The rest I will figure out in public.
